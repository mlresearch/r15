abstract: 'Informative Bayesian priors are often difficult to elicit, and when this
  is the case, modelers usually turn to noninformative or objective pri- ors. However,
  objective priors such as the Jef- freys and reference priors are not tractable to
  derive for many models of interest. We address this issue by proposing techniques
  for learn- ing reference prior approximations: we select a parametric family and
  optimize a black-box lower bound on the reference prior objective to find the member
  of the family that serves as a good approximation. We experimentally demonstrate
  the method’s effectiveness by re- covering Jeffreys priors and learning the Vari-
  ational Autoencoder’s reference prior.'
